Restorative Dentistry

Making smiles whole again

When a tooth is damaged, worn or missing, restorative dentistry rebuilds it — so you can chew properly, speak clearly and stop thinking about it.

What’s Included

What restorative dentistry covers

Restorative work spans a small filling through to a full set of dentures. The right answer depends on how much tooth is left, what the bite is doing to it, and what you want to spend now versus later.

How We Approach It

How we decide what to use

How much tooth is left

A small cavity takes a filling. A tooth with a crack or little structure left needs full coverage, or it will split.

What your bite is doing

Grinding and clenching break restorations. If that’s you, the plan includes protecting the work — usually a night splint.

What it costs over time

The cheapest option today isn’t always cheapest over ten years. We’ll set out both so the choice is genuinely yours.

How many visits you can spare

CEREC crowns finish in one appointment; lab-made work takes two. Both are good, and sometimes convenience decides it.

Who It Suits

Come in if

  • A tooth is broken, cracked or has a large old filling
  • You have amalgam fillings you’d rather were tooth-coloured
  • A crown or bridge has come loose or fallen out
  • You’re missing one or more teeth and chewing has changed
  • Your denture rocks, rubs or has broken
  • A tooth has had root canal treatment and hasn’t been crowned yet

Costs & Health Funds

What it costs, before it starts

Every treatment plan we give you carries its price, its inclusions and any conditions in writing before you agree to anything. Most health funds rebate on restorative treatment, and larger plans can be spread interest-free through Humm or DentiCare (lending criteria, fees and T&Cs apply).

Health funds on the spot

Bring your card and we claim through HICAPS at the desk, so you only pay the gap. All funds welcome, including HCF, Bupa, Medibank, nib, CBHS, Teachers Health, Defence Health, Australian Unity and GMHBA.
